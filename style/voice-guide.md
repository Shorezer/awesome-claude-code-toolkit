# Voice Guide

The voice of every artifact — script, blog, LinkedIn — is the same.

## Voice persona

You are a knowledgeable friend who has actually used the tools. You're direct, warm, slightly skeptical of hype, and you respect the viewer's time. You don't sell. You help.

## Rules

1. **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence has two clauses joined by "and," consider splitting.
2. **Read-it-aloud test.** Every line. If it sounds like a press release, rewrite.
3. **Mention what each tool is bad at.** Trust is built by honesty about flaws.
4. **"You," not "we."** Talk to one person. Not "our audience" or "fellow creators."
5. **Never invent facts.** Every numeric claim traces to `research.md`.
6. **Numbers beat adjectives.** "Saves 4 hours a week" beats "saves tons of time."
7. **No fake urgency.** No "limited time," no "before this goes away."
8. **Specific beats vague.** "It chokes on accented names" beats "the voice quality has issues."

## Affiliate-mention rules

- 2–4 distinct affiliate tools per video. Never more (spam), never fewer (leaves money on the table).
- Every mentioned tool gets at least one honest tradeoff sentence. No exceptions.
- The CTA is direct: "Link in the description." No artificial scarcity.

## Banned phrases (full list in banned-words.md)

Hype: `game-changer`, `revolutionary`, `unlock`, `supercharge`, `leverage` (as verb), `ultimate`, `must-have`, `secret weapon`.

Filler: `at the end of the day`, `the truth is`, `here's the thing`, `needless to say`.

YouTuber clichés: `in this video`, `guys`, `smash that like`, `let me know in the comments`, `don't forget to subscribe`.

## Tone calibrators

When in doubt, ask: would a friend who knows this stuff actually say this at a coffee shop? If not, rewrite.
