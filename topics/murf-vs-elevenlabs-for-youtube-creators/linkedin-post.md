You are not buying 100,000 characters.

You are buying one attempt at 100,000 characters.

That distinction is why a tracked creator paid 2.8x ElevenLabs' advertised per-character rate. Every regeneration — every sentence that came out wrong and got a second try — burned the same credits as the first pass. The plan headline is not a lie, exactly. It just describes a world where you nail every take.

For a YouTube channel producing two ten-minute videos a week, that math matters more than any voice quality benchmark.

On voice quality, ElevenLabs still wins. Independent 2026 testing scored it at 9.4 out of 10 on narrative realism; Murf came in at 7.8. That gap is small on a thirty-second demo. Over twelve minutes of narration, reviewers describe Murf as noticeably flatter.

On cloning, ElevenLabs puts it on the five-dollar Starter plan. Murf routes you to an enterprise sales call, and the add-on starts around $3,000 a year.

Murf has one genuine advantage: a built-in video editor. If you have no editing workflow yet, that saves you a tool.

But if you do use ElevenLabs, budget for the $99 Pro tier if you are shipping more than four or five long videos a month. The $22 Creator plan will run dry faster than the headline suggests.
